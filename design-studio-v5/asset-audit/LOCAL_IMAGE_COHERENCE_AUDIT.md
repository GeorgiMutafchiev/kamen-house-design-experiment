# KAMEN HOUSE — local concept image coherence audit

**Scope and evidence.** I visually inspected all 15 files in `public/images/*.webp` at their native or near-native size and checked dimensions, format and available embedded metadata. I compared them with `lib/content.ts`, the current image uses in `app/`, `design-studio-v5/PRODUCT_TRUTH.md`, and §30 of the owner-adopted V5 brief. These are generated **concept studies**, not photographs of a verified property, landscape, meal, route or guest experience. Visual observation below describes pixels; statements about whether depicted spaces belong to one building are explicitly hypotheses. Filenames and captions are not provenance.

## Coherence findings

1. **Place and architecture are only partially connected.** `house-concept.webp` supplies one convincing stone/timber vocabulary, while `room-corner-concept.webp`, `room-quiet-concept.webp` and `room-upper-concept.webp` show other roofs/buildings. Their relationship to the exterior is unknown. Eleven named rooms, several floor conditions, a courtyard, garden access and a shared kitchen cannot be mapped from the available images. This is a missing evidence chain, not proof of impossible architecture.
2. **Several room images do not carry their decision-critical promise.** The strongest direct mismatches are Room 01's depicted chair/narrow-looking bed versus copy about a window bench/double bed (`room-concept.webp`); Room 02's panorama versus lower-path outlook (`room-detail-concept.webp`); Room 07's standalone daybed image versus queen-plus-daybed occupancy (`room-corner-concept.webp`); and Room 09's visible stone roofs versus copy that says the valley view excludes village roofs (`room-upper-concept.webp`). Room 05's low headroom is also not apparent in `room-roof-concept.webp`. These should not serve as visual proof of bed, view or access claims.
3. **The seasonal and light story can work as a sequence but needs labels.** `house-concept.webp` and `path-concept.webp` read as wet autumn/dusk or mist; `room-garden-concept.webp`, `room-twin-concept.webp` and `room-last-concept.webp` show dense green daylight. `room-detail-concept.webp` has clear strong daylight; `room-long-concept.webp` has late warm light. The mix could depict separate days and seasons, but an unlabeled gallery would imply one coherent visit. Do not force color grading to disguise different conditions.
4. **Materials are a strength, with a risk of decorative repetition.** Rough stone, plaster, dark wood, woven rugs and striped textiles recur across almost every room. This makes a recognizable world, but the recurring similar bed/rug/window composition can make distinct rooms hard to compare. `room-twin-concept.webp`, `room-family-concept.webp` and `room-garden-concept.webp` provide more specific evidence; `room-roof-concept.webp` and `room-last-concept.webp` are less distinctive without text.
5. **Lens and capture language varies.** The exterior is a wide cinematic dusk view, hearth/path/Room 01 are portrait close or narrow views, and most room studies are 768 × 512 wide views with straight architectural framing. This can create a useful visual rhythm, but the eight small room files have less crop latitude for desktop full-bleed use. Their 768-pixel source width should constrain rendered size; do not upscale them to imply high-resolution room photography.
6. **People and hospitality are underrepresented.** `kitchen-concept.webp` is the sole image with a human presence, limited to hands. There is no dining table in use, staff/guest interaction or arrival sequence. The set shows preparation and rooms, but not the shared meal or how a visitor moves from path to house to table. These are visual gaps, not grounds to invent real guests.
7. **No reliable AI-artifact verdict follows from these files.** At inspected size I did not find a clear, repeatable anatomical or geometric defect that can be named with confidence. Small high-frequency textures, textiles, masonry and cookware remain susceptible to generated-image inconsistencies and should be checked at final display crops. The decisive issue is provenance and scene continuity, not a speculative artifact list.

## Photography / asset direction constraints

- Keep every concept image explicitly marked as a generated study in proximity to the image. Never describe a visible room, garden, meal, route or exterior as a verified amenity or documentary view. `PRODUCT_TRUTH.md` governs any room/stay claim.
- Use a single plausible physical vocabulary: local-feeling rough stone, limewashed plaster, dark worked timber, practical ironwork, wool/linen and working kitchen surfaces. When commissioning or generating further studies, lock a consistent exterior massing, roof, window profile, masonry color, room orientation and sightline map **before** adding scenes.
- Make room imagery answer what a visitor chooses on: bed arrangement, room scale, window outlook, bathroom/access condition and distinctive feature. When a concept crop cannot show the feature, avoid using it as proof. Commission a second view or state the limitation plainly. Prioritize Rooms 01, 02, 05, 07 and 09 for corrected visual evidence.
- Keep weather and season legible: wet autumn exterior and path can coexist with summer garden images when presented as separate seasonal studies. Do not assert they were captured on the same day. Preserve physically plausible light direction and window outlooks within each scene.
- Treat fire as working food infrastructure. Pair the tactile pot close-up (`hearth-concept.webp`) with preparation (`kitchen-concept.webp`) only as two unverified concept scenes until the stove/hearth relationship is resolved. If food is a core proposition, add table, serving and meal context; a flame alone cannot carry it.
- Let architectural shots remain architectural and food/people shots feel lived in. Keep restrained perspective, natural light and honest material texture. Avoid ultra-wide stretching, glossy resort lighting, fake documentary grain and decorative-only fire imagery. For any new people imagery, depict believable actions and obtain provenance/consent for real photography; do not imply generated faces are guests.
- Maintain a source manifest for each eventual asset: creator/source, generation or license status, intended scene, date/season if real, room/place mapping, approved crop, disclosure text, and known claims it can/cannot support. The current WebPs do not supply that provenance internally.
- At final layout size, inspect windows, roofs, railing/threshold lines, hands, utensils, textile repeats and joins between stone and timber. Reject visible geometry errors. Check both desktop and mobile crops, especially the 768 × 512 room studies.

## Gaps to resolve before a V5 champion can rely on these assets

**High priority:** a coherent house plan or visual continuity board; one honest room overview for each of the 11 concepts with bed/view claims aligned; corrected or qualified scenes for Rooms 01, 02, 07 and 09; clarity on the stove/open-hearth relationship; and a reliable asset provenance register. A generated concept set can inform art direction, but it cannot provide trust evidence for a live hospitality offer.

**Medium priority:** shared table / served food, path-to-door arrival, garden-to-room threshold, courtyard relationship, actual room access/stairs and bathrooms, and seasonal coverage where needed. The current single images cannot answer those visitor questions.

**Current use:** `house-concept.webp` repeats on Home, House and a Journal entry; `path-concept.webp` repeats on Home, Around and a Journal entry; `hearth-concept.webp` repeats on Food and a Journal entry. Repetition is useful for consistency, but these three scenes are carrying several distinct page jobs. Any new sequence should broaden the story with connected views rather than only additional atmospheric landscapes.

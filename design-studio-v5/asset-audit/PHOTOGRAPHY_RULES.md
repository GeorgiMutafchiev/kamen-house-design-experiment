# KAMEN HOUSE — photography and concept-asset rules

Use this rule set when selecting, generating, commissioning or cropping imagery. It is derived from `PRODUCT_TRUTH.md`, the V5 photography gate (§30), and `LOCAL_IMAGE_COHERENCE_AUDIT.md`. It describes a visual direction for an **imagined hospitality concept**; it is not a property survey or a claim that a photo shoot has occurred.

## Evidence boundary

| Status | What can be said | Image treatment |

## Scene continuity rules

1. **Architecture.** Define one continuity sheet before creating connected scenes: exterior massing, number of visible levels, roof pitch/material, chimney, entrance, window shapes, courtyard, garden, kitchen location and room sightlines. Every new interior view must plausibly fit that sheet. Current `house-concept.webp` offers only one angle; roofs visible in `room-corner-concept.webp`, `room-quiet-concept.webp` and `room-upper-concept.webp` cannot yet be placed. Do not imply those images prove one building.
2. **Materials.** Carry a restrained palette of rough grey stone, pale limewash, dark aged timber, useful ironwork and tactile wool/linen from the current set. Permit room-specific textiles and objects so eleven rooms remain distinguishable. Avoid identical rugs, bed throws or generic rustic props repeated mechanically. Material joins, wood grain and masonry courses must remain physically credible across views.
3. **Rhodopes geography.** Use the stated Rhodopes setting as a *brief*, not a geolocation claim. Aim for plausible forested slopes, layered ridges, stone paths and seasonal vegetation. Do not identify a peak, village, route marker, plant species or precise viewpoint from a concept image. `path-concept.webp` can signal wet woodland; it cannot validate a named trail or safety advice. Any real route/location image needs a verified place and source record.
4. **Season and weather.** Build scene groups with a declared season and weather, then preserve them within a sequence. `house-concept.webp` and `path-concept.webp` suggest wet autumn/mist; `room-garden-concept.webp`, `room-twin-concept.webp` and `room-last-concept.webp` show stronger green growth. They may represent separate visits. Caption seasonal shifts when juxtaposed; do not grade foliage or weather to imply one day of capture.
5. **Lens and perspective.** Prefer a human standing viewpoint with straight verticals and believable room proportions. As a shooting/generation target, use roughly 28–50 mm full-frame-equivalent views for spaces and 50–85 mm for details; these are guidance, **not metadata claims** about current files. Avoid extreme wide-angle expansion that makes a small room appear larger. Include at least one unambiguous overview before a tactile detail on room pages.
6. **Light and color.** Let daylight direction agree with the stated window orientation and time of day; let stove/fire light fall on nearby surfaces. Keep cool mist outside and warm practical light inside without turning all rooms amber. Retain shadow detail and material color. `room-detail-concept.webp` is bright and clear while `house-concept.webp` is dusk; those are separate conditions until tied by a documented sequence.
7. **People.** Show believable hospitality actions only when they clarify the experience: arrival, cooking, serving, sitting at a shared table or walking. The only current human presence is cropped hands in `kitchen-concept.webp`; there are no evidenced guests or hosts. For real photographs, record subject consent/release. For generated figures, disclose their status and never describe them as actual staff or guests. Avoid decorative, anonymous lifestyle figures.
8. **Rooms, windows and claims.** A room image may support only what is visibly clear. Show bed configuration, scale, primary outlook and relevant access/bathroom condition in a truthful sequence; do not crop away a feature that the room copy asks visitors to rely on. Current priority conflicts: `room-concept.webp` does not establish Room 01's double bed/window bench; `room-detail-concept.webp` shows a panorama rather than Room 02's lower-path outlook; `room-corner-concept.webp` does not show Room 07's queen bed; `room-upper-concept.webp` contains stone roofs despite Room 09's roof-excluding view copy. `room-roof-concept.webp` does not demonstrate Room 05's restricted headroom. Resolve with a revised concept scene or qualify the copy/image relationship; never treat the file as verification.
9. **Food and fire.** Show fire as the means of making and sharing food: prep, cooking, serving and table. `kitchen-concept.webp` shows an enclosed wood stove, while `hearth-concept.webp` shows a pot by open embers. A wider connecting view or continuity plan is needed before implying both are the same fire setup. Avoid flames used only as a mood device and avoid presenting generated meals or service as current operations.
10. **AI artifact gate.** Inspect proposed generated images at native resolution and final desktop/mobile render size. Check hands, cookware handles, bed geometry, window mullions, roof lines, stair/threshold levels, stone/timber joins, textile repeats, shadows and reflections. Reject clear geometry or anatomy errors and any view that breaks the continuity sheet. The current audit found no confidently identifiable repeatable artifact; do not invent a defect to make a rejection case.

## Crop and production checks

- Keep a master image plus approved desktop and mobile crops. Test whether the room's decision-critical feature survives each crop, with text overlays and focal points considered. A mobile crop of `room-family-concept.webp` must retain both the main bed and bunks if it is used to explain occupancy; a crop of `room-corner-concept.webp` still cannot establish a queen bed.
- Do not stretch the eight 768 × 512 room studies into large, high-density full-bleed slots. They are suitable for modest display until replaced by higher-resolution, coherent scenes. Check sharpness and compression in the actual rendered size rather than assuming WebP format is sufficient.
- Keep per-asset records for origin/creator, generated-versus-photographic status, rights or license, intended room/place, scene continuity group, season/time if known, disclosure, approved crops and claims the image may support. No camera, location or creator provenance was exposed in the current WebPs' inspected metadata.
- Apply the same concept disclosure on Home, Rooms, Food, House, Around and Journal. Reuse of `house-concept.webp`, `path-concept.webp` and `hearth-concept.webp` across pages is acceptable, but a future sequence should add connected views rather than repeat one image as proof of several experiences.

## Replacement priorities

1. **Before any image-led room decision flow:** resolve the Room 01, 02, 07 and 09 claim conflicts named above; show Room 05's low-beam constraint honestly. Commission/generate room overviews that demonstrate bed, view and scale before adding atmosphere.
2. **Before a coherent property story:** establish the continuity sheet and connected exterior, entrance, courtyard, garden and kitchen views. Resolve the stove/open-hearth relationship. These may remain disclosed concepts during this experiment.
3. **Before relying on food as a differentiator:** add a credible served-table sequence linked to preparation and fire. Do not substitute a flame close-up for hospitality.
4. **Before making real-world location, access or booking claims:** replace concept scenes with verified, rights-cleared documentary evidence of the property and relevant routes; validate copy against the actual site and operations. Until then, keep imagery and enquiry language explicitly conceptual.

# KAMEN HOUSE — product truth

## Product and evidence boundary

This repository presents an **imagined hospitality concept**, not a verified operating property. The existing content describes a small independent mountain house in the Rhodopes with eleven distinct concept rooms, a shared fire kitchen, walking, and a simple stay enquiry preview. The concept images are generated studies and are explicitly labelled as such in the current site. Room details, meals, timings, route descriptions and the house itself are narrative concept material; they are not evidence of current operations.

Confirmed for this experiment: the site has eleven named room concepts, pages for Rooms, Food, House, Around, Journal, Find Us and Stay, and an enquiry interface that does **not** transmit a booking. The exact address/map pin, live availability, rates, operational kitchen, reservations and real guest experiences are unverified. The website must not imply a booking was completed.

## Why it should exist

**Internal product sentence:** KAMEN HOUSE should give a small number of guests a grounded mountain stay where a good room, a shared fire-cooked table and unhurried time outdoors feel like one lived place.

This is a design and product filter, not proposed marketing copy. Guests should understand the room scale, the food identity, the surrounding landscape and the practical path to a stay without having to decode a visual experiment.

## Commercial proposition

The eventual primary action is a **truthful stay enquiry** for a specific room or dates. In this isolated concept, the honest action is to inspect rooms and start the clearly labelled enquiry preview. The site cannot claim live availability, a confirmed reservation or successful delivery of an enquiry. High-intent visitors need fast Rooms and Stay access; medium-intent visitors need enough room, food, landscape and practical detail to decide whether to ask; discovery visitors need immediate context.

## Brand constraints

The house should feel physical, specific, inhabited and contemporary: stone, timber, weather, fire, table and paths as parts of one experience. It should not look like a generic luxury resort, a beige boutique template, an architecture portfolio, an AI-builder demo, a folklore costume or a conversion dashboard. The fire kitchen must feel like food and hospitality, not a decorative flame motif. Mountain imagery must support the sense of a place to stay, not carry a weak interface by itself.
